# Players tab (League HQ)

Oct 29, 2026 refresh: `scripts/players-toolbar.js` + `styles/players-toolbar.css`, both added by `prepare-site.mjs` (index.html untouched). The build routes both toolbar renderers (`hj40RefreshControls` and `hjDirectoryControlsHTML`, which the panel uses when league data refreshes) through `window.hjPlayersToolbarHTML`, calls `window.hjPlayersToolbarSync()` after every toolbar patch, and exposes the directory helpers it needs as `window.HJ40_API` (`state`, `actualWeek`, `actualSeason`, `projection`, `gameStarted`, `gameState`, `availableWeeks`, `currentWeek`, `refreshControls`, `ensureSeason`, `publish`, `labels`). Every existing handler (position, availability, team, manager, search, PFF / Market / stat-rail sorts, reset) is reused: the new markup carries the same data attributes.

## Sticky toolbar (three rows, no taller than the old one)
- It pins under the site nav and the folder tabs (`top: nav height + folder-tab height`, z-index 70), the same place the old `.hq49-controls` toolbar pinned. Pinning it under the nav alone puts it behind the folder tabs.
- Row 1: search ("Search players") · All / Free agents toggle · a small Trend button (`data-hq40-sort-stat="trend"`, the Sleeper 24h adds/drops sort; tap again to flip) · the sort slot. In the default order the slot is the hint "Tap any stat to sort"; once a sort is chosen it becomes a navy chip: ↓/↑ + the sort's name (tap to flip the direction) and × to clear the sort.
- Row 2: Team, Manager, Season (2026/2025) and period (Season Totals, Season Averages, Week 1…n) dropdowns, each as wide as its words (Season is the narrow one), plus a red × (`data-hq40-reset`, the site's own reset, which here also sets projections back to E) whenever a filter is set or the order is not the default.
- Row 3: position chips (All, QB, RB, WR, TE, Flex, K, DST) and the E | V switch: ESPN or Vegas projections for the whole list.
- There is no Sort By dropdown: sorting is done by tapping the FPTS / PROJ buttons, the PFF / Market boxes, the Trend button or a stat in the rail.
- The default order is this week's projections, descending. The old "Trend 24h" default is gone: `prepare-player-stat-bars.mjs` no longer sets `statSort:'trend'` at load and `hj40DefaultPlayerView` (reset) no longer sets it either. The waiver-wire "open" link still opens the tab sorted by trend on purpose.
- The site patches the toolbar in place. A dropdown or search box the reader already changed keeps showing its old value after a patch (browsers ignore markup changes on an edited field), so `hjPlayersToolbarSync` sets each field to what the new markup says.

## Card tiles
The card keeps its original THIS WEEK · SEASON · PFF GRADE · MARKET strip (same markup and styling; the ⓘ buttons stay; the strip's right end is rounded like its left end). Inside THIS WEEK and SEASON the big number and sub line are replaced by two small side-by-side buttons, **FPTS** and **PROJ**, set in the stat bar's own type (Public Sans 9px: label 400 uppercase, value 850). Each is a sort target (`data-hj-sort="weekActual|week|seasonActual|season"`); the sorted one looks like the stat bar's sorted stat (beige #efe9d8 fill, brass underline) and its text does not change. Tapping it again flips the direction.

The PROJ label carries a letter for the source of the number shown (brass E = ESPN, navy V = Vegas). It is only a label: the whole button sorts. The source is chosen once for the list with the E | V switch, and the projection sort uses the same source (falling back to the other source when a player has none). There is deliberately no per-player flip inside the button: a small toggle nested in a sort button gets hit by taps meant for the button (phones snap a tap to the nearest small target).

New sort keys `weekActual` / `seasonActual`, and both projection keys in both directions, are applied by an outermost wrapper of `hjDirectoryFilteredRows` (players without a value go last).

On phones (< 720px) the strip is split by content, not in equal quarters: 90 : 90 : 82 : 62 (THIS WEEK, SEASON, PFF, MARKET). At 360px that is the least each needs (two six-digit values with room on each side / "PFF GRADE ⓘ" / "MARKET ⓘ"); with equal quarters the two-number boxes clip. Below 381px the gaps between boxes are 2px. PFF and MARKET always stack title · value · rank, also on cards that have only one of them (K, 2025). On desktop the box titles share one top line.

## List behaviour
- "D/ST" is written as "DST" in the card markup before it reaches the page. (The site's clean-up pass otherwise shows "Seahawks D/ST" for about 0.4 s whenever DST cards render.)
- A sort or filter tap is followed by up to two hydration re-renders with identical HTML. The list is only replaced when its markup changed, so a quick second tap is not lost mid-rebuild and the list does not blink.
- The site's live patcher keeps the card nearest the top of the screen in place when the list is rebuilt, which suits live refreshes. After a new sort or filter that card has moved, and the page used to chase it (jumps of up to 2,000px). When the order or filters change, the list now shows from #1 right under the toolbar (only if the reader had scrolled into the list); live refreshes keep the old anchoring.
- Known, unchanged: with Season 2025 selected, the THIS WEEK / SEASON boxes still show this season's numbers while the stat bar shows 2025 (same as before the refresh).

## Stat bar numbers (Oct 29 audit)
Every number on the stat bar was recomputed from the source files (nflverse weekly stats, snap counts and team stats; ESPN's D/ST weekly stats; ffopportunity) for every player, for Season Totals, Season Averages and single weeks, in 2026 and 2025, and compared with the cards. Fixes, all build swaps in `prepare-site.mjs` unless noted:
- **Season Averages divide by games played on offense.** The nflverse weekly file has a row for every game on a player's team schedule, including games he sat out (all zeros); the average divided by all of them (Josh Jacobs: 20.10 in one game showed 2.87; 217 of 572 averages were wrong, e.g. Lamar Jackson 11.30 instead of 19.77). Every per-game stat (yards, catches, TDs…) uses the same game count. A week counts when the player logged offensive snaps or recorded any stat or points (`hj40Played`, `hj40SnapPlayed`, `hj40EspnPlayed`).
- **SNP%.** A team's offensive snaps were estimated as the largest snaps ÷ snap% over its players; snap% is rounded to two decimals, so one snap at "0.01" made a 100-snap game and every SNP% came out low (Gibbs Week 7: 61% instead of 90%). Now taken from the player with the most snaps (`hjSnapMap`). Season / average SNP% leaves out games with no offensive snaps (`hjSnapPercent`; a single such week still shows 0%).
- **Same-name players.** Snap rows were matched by name only, so the Vikings' Justin Jefferson also got a Browns linebacker's rows (Week 5 SNP% 45% instead of 89%). Defenders with no offensive snaps are left out of the snap lookup.
- **Initials.** "D.K. Metcalf" (snap counts) and "DK Metcalf" (ESPN, stats) never matched, so D.K. Metcalf, D.J. Moore and others had no SNP%. `pcBaseName` joins single letters, on every side of every comparison.
- **Nicknames.** Snap counts list Kenneth Gainwell, Zonovan Knight, Chigoziem Okonkwo, Gabriel Davis…; stats use Kenny, Bam, Chig, Gabe. An offensive player's snap rows with no name match go to the one player with the same last name, team and week who has no snaps of his own.
- **D/ST.** TKL, FF, QB HIT and KR TD were summed from defensive players' rows, which the site never loads (it keeps only QB/RB/WR/TE/K), so they always read 0 or "—". TKL (109), FF (106), KR TD (101) and ST TD (93+101+102) now come from ESPN's D/ST weekly stats, which the site already loads; DEF TD now uses 103+104 (checked against nflverse team totals; the old stat 105 disagreed in 30 team-weeks). QB HIT, which ESPN does not have, comes from the nflverse team file (below).
- **D/ST data on first open.** A season's data could be built before the player pool had its 32 D/ST entries; the list then stayed empty for a minute and every defense showed FPTS "—" and 0 sacks when DST was tapped. `scripts/player-advanced-data.js` fetches them again until they arrive and republishes the season (`HJ40_API.publish`).
- **2025.** The embedded 2025 data has no passing air yards (AIR YD was 0 for every QB) and lacks 36 skill players' snap rows (D.J. Moore, D.K. Metcalf, Kenneth Gainwell…). `data/season-supplement-2025.json` (written by the publish workflow, see XFP below) fills both (merged in `pcLoadSeason` / `pcLoadSnaps` for finished seasons).
- **Snap race.** Snap counts often finished loading before the Players state existed; their event was dropped and the state, built without snaps, was not reloaded for a minute, so SNP% (and the games-played count) went blank for everyone. A state built without snaps takes the snap counts already loaded (`hj40Publish`).
- **Target / air-yard share.** Computed over the games he played (zero-target games count). A season share no longer goes blank when one week (a live ESPN row) lacks share data (`hjUsageSummary`).
- Remaining differences are by design: a few players' target counts differ by one (and with them TGT %, catch %, yards per target) because the site prefers ESPN's official stats to nflverse's; fullbacks have no TGT % / AIR % (the site's nflverse feed keeps QB/RB/WR/TE/K rows, not FB).

## Player ids (ESPN ↔ nflverse)
ESPN rows and nflverse rows used to be merged, and players matched to their stats, by name. A player the two sources spell differently (Hollywood / Marquise Brown, Drew / Andrew Ogletree, Josh / Joshua Palmer, Irv / Irvin Charles, Jalen Cropper / Moreno-Cropper, Matt / Matthew Hibner) had each week twice, counted twice in the defense-vs-position tables, and two players with one name (Josh Johnson) shared stats. `data/player-ids.json` (nflverse players table: nflverse / ESPN / Pro Football Reference ids, rebuilt daily by `scripts/refresh-nflverse-extras.mjs`) is loaded before any merge (`hjLoadPlayerIds`, waits at most 4 s). Rows merge by nflverse id (`pcMergeSeasonActualRows`, each merged row carries `hj_gsis`); a player's rows (`byGsis`) and snaps (`snapsByGsis`, from the snap rows' `pfr_player_id`, which the workflow now keeps) are found by id. Name matching remains only for a player the table does not know. Result: 0 duplicate player-weeks (18 before), no player with another player's rows.

## OPP RK
OPP RK and every "(n)" opponent rank on the site use ESPN's own ratings (`positionAgainstOpponent` in the league's `kona_player_info`, league scoring), fetched with the current week and cached 30 minutes (`scripts/opponent-rank-updates.js`). The site's own table (`pcDvp`) is the fallback; it disagreed with ESPN for 61 of 160 defense-position pairs (duplicate player-weeks, a different game count, ties ranked differently; Carolina vs TE: 9th instead of ESPN's 18th). Checked: all 192 pairs (QB, RB, WR, TE, K, D/ST) equal ESPN's.

## XFP (expected fantasy points)
## Advanced stats (back of the bar)
After the box-score stats and before ROST % / START % / TREND / OPP RK: QB: XFP · RB: XFP, TGT %, RZ OPP · WR / TE: XFP, TGT %, AIR %, RZ OPP, SEP. All sort like every other stat and follow the period.
- **TGT %**: share of his team's targets in the games he played. **AIR %**: share of his team's air yards (how far downfield the throws to him travelled, caught or not). Both from nflverse.
- **RZ OPP**: carries plus targets with the ball inside the opponent's 20 (nflverse play-by-play; two-point tries and nullified plays left out); per game over the same games as FPTS.
- **SEP**: average separation from the nearest defender when the ball arrives, yards (NFL Next Gen Stats). Totals and averages show the season to date; a week appears only when he met the NGS minimum.
- **XFP**: see below.

XFP is for QB, RB, WR and TE (not K or DST: there is no model for them). It is what an average player would score with the same targets, carries and throws, given where and how deep they were, in league scoring: ffopportunity's full-PPR figure plus the league's +0.5 per expected catch for tight ends. FPTS above XFP means the player finished better than his opportunity; below means the opportunity was there. It follows the period (season total, per game over the same games as FPTS, one week) and sorts like every other stat. The current week has no XFP until the source updates after the week (usually Tuesday or Wednesday).

Data: `scripts/refresh-nflverse-extras.mjs` writes `data/nflverse-extras-<season>.json` (sections `expected`, `defense`, `redzone`, `separation`) on every site publication (workflow step "Refresh nflverse extras"); a section whose source fails keeps the last published copy. The same step writes the previous season's files (`nflverse-extras-2025.json`, `season-supplement-2025.json`); a finished season does not change, so the published copies are reused and only rebuilt when missing. Locally: `node scripts/refresh-nflverse-extras.mjs --static 2025 <dir>`. `scripts/player-advanced-data.js` (inlined by `prepare-site.mjs`) loads the file for the season shown and attaches it to the Players data (`window.hjNflExtras`, `window.hjXfpFor`, `window.hjRzFor`, `window.hjSepFor`). Players are matched by nflverse id. Play-by-play (7–20 MB) is fetched again only when the published red-zone section is over three hours old. Test: `scripts/test-nflverse-extras.mjs`.
